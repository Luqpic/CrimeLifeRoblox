Crimelife Compound

The new Crimelife tycoon base. It replaces the old 2023 build and the earlier tower-style HQ blueprint. It's a walled compound that looks like a sleek, modern logistics company from the street. The rooms wrap around an open central Yard with a player spawn and a fight pit. A drive lane runs down the east side, and a skybridge links the main building to a Watchtower.

It keeps every room from the old blueprint. It moves to a flatter layout with a ground floor, an upper floor, a roof terrace and a bunker under the yard. Use the reference screenshot for mood and massing only: the ring of rooms around a yard, the cut-off corner, the bridged tower. Don't copy it one to one.

Style: modern industrial. Clean, sharp and well kept, never run-down or scrappy.

Coordinates

  • Every number uses one plot coordinate system. The origin is the plot's north-west corner, +X runs east, +Z runs south. Units are studs.
  • Put the plot model's pivot on the NW corner so the numbers become offsets in Studio.
  • Y = 0 is the top of the ground slab. B1 runs from −24 to 0, G from 0 to 20, F2 from 20 to 40. The roof terrace floor is at 40 with a parapet to 44.
  • Exterior walls are 2 thick and interior walls 1. Room edges on the plans are wall centrelines.

Legend

Look and materials

Think of a high-end logistics headquarters or a modern warehouse office, not a junkyard. Clean lines, big flat surfaces and a few sharp details per room.

  • Walls: smooth light-grey concrete, with dark charcoal metal cladding panels on the upper floor.
  • Frames and trims: matte black steel window frames, door frames, railings and roof edges.
  • Glass: large floor-to-ceiling panels at the entrance, the cut corner, the Penthouse and the gallery.
  • Accents: warm wood on the yard deck and terrace, warm-white LED strip lights under roof edges and along the gallery.
  • Colour: greys, charcoal and black with one accent colour (safety yellow) on doors, bollards and markings.
  • Avoid: rust, broken parts, piles of junk, mismatched scrap panels and graffiti.

How players buy the base

Most tycoons have buttons on the floor that players step on to buy the next piece. This one doesn't. We're reusing the build system from Outbreak Tycoon, another game by the same developer. It's already scripted, so you only build. In-game it works like this:

  1. The player opens a BUILD menu on their screen. It lists every purchase as a card, grouped by floor (Ground, Upper, Roof, Bunker, Outside) and by section (Economy, Structure, Interior, Weapons, Decoration, Facilities).
  2. Picking a card moves the camera to that spot in the base. A blue see-through hologram shows what will be built there.
  3. The player presses BUY. The pieces of that purchase drop in from a few studs above and land in place, one layer after another.

What that means for your build

  • Build the whole base finished, in place. The scripts hide anything a player hasn't bought yet, so you never build "before" and "after" versions.
  • One Model per purchase. Every room's structure, each machine and each furniture set is its own Model with a plain name, like Scrap Foundry, Foundry Droppers or Bar Furniture. Group the Models in folders by floor. Split each room from the schedules below into its structure (floor, walls, ceiling, lights) plus a separate Model for its machines and one for its furniture.
  • A few layers inside each Model. Put the floor, walls and ceiling, and the props in separate sub-models. Those are the layers the drop-in animation lands one after another, so a single Model of 400 loose parts looks bad.
  • A camera spot for each purchase. Add one invisible, anchored, non-colliding part named CameraPoint where the menu camera should look from. Put it a little outside and above the purchase, with a clear view of it.
  • Nothing on the floor for buying. No pads, buttons, price signs or boards.

Build in order, never remove

Each purchase only adds to what's already there. Nothing is deleted, swapped or capped.

  • Floor, walls, ceiling. Each floor's structure includes its ceiling slab, and that slab is the next floor's floor. The ground floor is roofed from the start, and the upper floor builds straight on top of it. The upper floor's ceiling is the Roof Terrace floor.
  • Exterior walls come with each floor's structure. Upper-floor walls stand on the slab edge, so there is no parapet on the ground slab to get in the way.
  • No early fence. The base plate edge marks the plot until the perimeter wall is bought.
  • Empty rooms still read as rooms. A room's structure brings its floor finish, walls and lights. Furniture and machines are separate purchases.
